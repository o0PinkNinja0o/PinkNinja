IThe Host+
PERSONA // THE HOST

PERSONA FILE // I

Expanded dossier

A place for the details behind the introduction: the habits, history and little contradictions that make this persona feel real.

Core idea

What does The Host invite people into? Note what they want the room to feel like, what they hope visitors notice, and what they would rather keep out of sight.

Appearance & symbols

Describe their silhouette, clothing, colours and memorable objects. Record what each visual detail says about welcome, control, comfort or performance.

Temperament & habits

How do they behave when things go smoothly, and what changes when a guest surprises them? Add gestures, rituals, strengths, flaws and private anxieties.

Voice & point of view

Collect characteristic phrases, speaking rhythms and subjects they return to. What do they assume about a stranger before that stranger has said a word?

History & connections

Write down where their idea of hospitality came from, who taught them how to host, and which relationships make their welcoming act complicated.

Story seeds

What could make The Host leave the door unopened? What happens when a guest refuses the rules, stays too long or offers to take over?

IIThe Guest+
PERSONA // THE GUEST

PERSONA FILE // II

Expanded dossier

Keep the arrival, the visit and everything left unsaid together in one growing set of notes.

Core idea

Why has The Guest arrived, and what are they hoping to find? Note what they say they need alongside the thing they may actually be looking for.

Appearance & symbols

Describe what they brought with them, what they are still carrying and what they leave at the door. Record their silhouette, palette and visual motifs.

Temperament & habits

How quickly do they feel at home? Add their small courtesies, awkward habits, boundaries, unexpected strengths and the situations that make them retreat.

Voice & point of view

How does The Guest ask for something? Keep sample phrases, conversational tells and the topics they dodge when someone asks how the journey went.

History & connections

Record where they came from, what they are leaving behind and how they know The Host. Note what they owe, what they offer and what they misunderstand.

Story seeds

What would persuade them to stay? What might make them leave without saying goodbye, or invite someone else to come along?

IIIThe Spectator+
PERSONA // THE SPECTATOR

PERSONA FILE // III

Expanded dossier

File observations, blind spots and the moment watching becomes a choice to take part.

Core idea

What keeps The Spectator at the edge of the scene? Note what they are trying to understand, and whether observing feels safer, kinder or simply more interesting.

Appearance & symbols

Describe where they stand or sit, what they look through, and what they do with their hands. Gather recurring shapes, framing devices and visual cues.

Temperament & habits

What do they notice first that everyone else misses? Add their routines, assumptions, loyalties and the kinds of events that finally draw them in.

Voice & point of view

Write down how they narrate events, what they call evidence and when an observation turns into a judgement. Keep a few characteristic lines.

History & connections

Note how they learned to watch rather than participate. Who do they follow, protect or misunderstand from a distance?

Story seeds

What would The Spectator risk being seen doing? What changes when the scene they are watching notices them back?

IVThe Dreamer+
PERSONA // THE DREAMER

PERSONA FILE // IV

Expanded dossier

Gather the images, wishes and practical details that let this persona move between imagining and doing.

Core idea

What does The Dreamer keep imagining? Note the wish at the centre of it, what the dream makes possible and what it helps them avoid.

Appearance & symbols

Describe the shapes, textures and colours that belong to their inner world. Which objects appear in dreams, and which have followed them into waking life?

Temperament & habits

Record how they respond to interruption, uncertainty and routine. Add ways they capture ideas, lose track of time or surprise themselves by following through.

Voice & point of view

How do they explain an idea that has not happened yet? Keep the comparisons, unfinished sentences and sudden certainties that sound unmistakably like them.

History & connections

Write down where their most persistent hope began, who encourages it and who asks them to be practical. Note which relationships ground or unsettle them.

Story seeds

What happens when a dream becomes achievable? What would The Dreamer do if reality offered something better, stranger or less tidy than the plan?

VThe Stand-In+
PERSONA // THE STAND-IN

PERSONA FILE // V

Expanded dossier

Track the role they are asked to play, the role they choose and the person underneath both.

Core idea

Whom or what is The Stand-In replacing? Note who made the request, what everyone expects and what the stand-in hopes will happen instead.

Appearance & symbols

Describe what they borrow, imitate or deliberately change. Record the features that make the resemblance convincing and the details that give them away.

Temperament & habits

How much of the role do they perform, and where do they draw the line? Add tells, adaptations, private opinions and moments when their own nature breaks through.

Voice & point of view

Note whose words they repeat, what they change and how their own voice sounds when nobody is listening. Keep useful phrases and revealing slips.

History & connections

Record their relationship to the person being represented, the people who rely on the performance and anyone who sees through it.

Story seeds

What if the original returns, the performance works too well or the role begins to feel more comfortable than being recognised?

CHARACTER FILE // 01

Expanded dossier

A running record of Libet's design, personality, history and place in the wider cast. Add as much detail as the character needs.

At a glance

Summarise who Libet is, what they want most and the first impression they tend to leave. Add their role in the story and any names, titles or nicknames they use.

Appearance & design

Record height, silhouette, posture, clothing, palette and materials. Note how the design changes with mood, circumstance or time, and keep the meaning behind recurring visual motifs.

Personality & everyday life

Describe strengths, flaws, comforts, irritations and small routines. What are they like when relaxed, under pressure, alone or around someone they trust?

Voice & mannerisms

Keep sample dialogue, favourite expressions, speaking rhythm and body-language tells. Note what Libet says to be polite, what they mean, and what they leave unsaid.

History & motivations

Write the important events in order, the experiences that shaped their outlook and the current goal driving them forward. Include unresolved questions and details still being developed.

Relationships & story role

Map allies, rivals, family, acquaintances and complicated connections. Note what each person brings out in Libet, where trust stands and how those relationships might change.

Abilities, limits & trivia

List skills, special abilities and the rules or costs attached to them. Add weaknesses, favourite things, useful facts, design references and details that do not fit elsewhere.

Open questions & future notes

Collect plot ideas, alternate designs, continuity reminders and questions to revisit. Keep discarded possibilities here too, with a note about why they did not fit.

CHARACTER FILE // 02

Expanded dossier

Keep the practical facts, personality notes and odd little discoveries about Bucket Buddy together as the character grows.

At a glance

Introduce Bucket Buddy, their role in the cast and what makes them recognisable. Note what the name means to them and what a new person should notice first.

Appearance & design

Describe the bucket and every other part of the design: shape, scale, texture, colour, wear and movement. Record what can be added, removed or changed, and why.

Personality & everyday life

Write down their usual mood, best qualities, messiest habits and favourite ways to spend a day. How do they behave when they are excited, embarrassed or left out?

Voice & mannerisms

Note how Bucket Buddy communicates, including sounds, gestures, expressions or silence. Keep memorable lines and the tiny movements that make a reaction unmistakable.

History & motivations

Record how Bucket Buddy came to be, what they remember and what they are still trying to understand. Add present-day goals, worries and the reason each one matters.

Relationships & story role

Map their closest bonds, unlikely friendships and points of friction. What do other characters rely on Bucket Buddy for, and what support do they need in return?

Abilities, limits & trivia

List useful skills and their boundaries, plus favourite objects, dislikes, surprising talents and amusing facts. Add any rules the story should stay consistent about.

Open questions & future notes

Save possible scenes, new design ideas, continuity notes and mysteries to answer later. Leave room for unexpected details that only appear while drawing.
